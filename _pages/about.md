I am a **Postdoctoral Research Scholar at Mass Eye and Ear and Harvard Medical School**, specializing in computational and experimental solid mechanics, biomechanics, and biomedical engineering.

My research focuses on understanding and predicting the mechanical behavior, deformation, and failure of biological tissues and complex biomechanical systems. I integrate finite-element (FE) modeling, nonlinear constitutive modeling, experimental mechanics, medical imaging, uncertainty quantification, and machine learning to develop experimentally informed computational models.

## Research Interests

- Computational and Experimental Solid Mechanics
- Middle-Ear Biomechanics and Hearing Mechanics
- Biological Tissue Damage and Failure
- Nonlinear Finite-Element Modeling
- Experimental Mechanics and Model Validation
- Uncertainty Quantification and Machine Learning

## Current Research

My current research focuses on the biomechanics of the human hearing system, particularly the mechanical response of middle-ear structures under intense acoustic loading and surgical manipulation.

I develop subject-specific finite-element models informed by high-resolution imaging and experimental measurements to investigate tissue deformation, nonlinear behavior, mechanical injury, and functional impairment.

A central goal of my research is to establish predictive computational frameworks to identify injury mechanisms, assess the functional consequences of tissue damage, and contribute to safer otologic surgical procedures.

## Research Background

I received my PhD in Systems Design Engineering from the University of Waterloo. Before joining Mass Eye and Ear and Harvard Medical School, I conducted postdoctoral research at the University of Waterloo.

My previous research has included computational and experimental investigations of soft-tissue puncture, fracture and damage, nonlinear material behavior, and biomechanical systems.

## Future Research Directions

My long-term goal is to establish an independent research program integrating computational mechanics, experimental characterization, and data-driven modeling to investigate tissue injury and its consequences for biomechanical function.

While my current research emphasizes middle-ear biomechanics, I aim to extend these approaches to other mechanically sensitive biological tissues and structures, including the inner ear and other systems where nonlinear material behavior, complex loading conditions, and mechanical injury influence physiological function.
